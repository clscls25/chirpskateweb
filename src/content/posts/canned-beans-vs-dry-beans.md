I've always wondered which was cheaper, dried or canned beans. I never bothered to actually measure everything. Until today! 

I assumed the dry beans were going to be cheaper overall. You have to do a lot more work, soak, cook, there had to be a payoff.

I bought 2 cans of Trader Joe's organic garbanzo beans for $1.19 each. I also bought some organic dry garbanzo beans at a local grocery store for $3.79/lb.

## Time to prep the beans

I soaked 2 cups of dry garbanzos which weighed 14.1 ounces dry. At $3.79/lb these 2 cups cost about $3.34. After cooking it weighed 34.3 ounces, and comes out to 9.7 cents/ounce ($1.55/lb). Wow only $1.55/lb, tastes good, nutritious, healthy, beans should be a staple for a lot of people.

The canned garbanzos said it weighed 15.5 ounces and I assumed that was just the beans but that weight actually includes the water in the can too. The canned beans drained weighed 9.6 ounces. At $1.19 for 9.6 ounces that comes out to 12.4 cents/ounce ($1.98/lb). Still good, and under $2/lb.

## The price for convenience

It's hard to beat the convenience of canned beans, no soak time, no cooking, just open the can and rinse and you're ready to go. This convenience in this case costs about 43 cents per pound (about 28% more) for ready to go beans. Not bad, and not going to break the bank.

The canned Trader Joe's beans are surely some of the cheaper organic canned beans available. I've seen cans cost $2-4 regularly at various grocery stores. At $2/can it would be 20.1 cents/ounce ($3.22/lb), $3/can 31.3 cents/ounce ($5.00/lb), and $4/can 41.7 cents/ounce ($6.67/lb).

## The bean size was quite different

Of course there are numerous varieties of chickpeas. The canned Trader Joe's beans happen to be a larger variety, and the dry variety was smaller in this case. Both tasted about the same. You can probably argue dry taste better than canned but it's not a huge difference. Of note, they do add some salt to the canned beans so if you're watching your blood pressure or just get enough salt elsewhere, dry wins in that regard.

## Dried beans in bulk can be even cheaper

$3.79/lb is a good price for organic dried beans. I've seen specialty organic dried beans go for $6.00/lb. If you buy in bulk there are a few USA organic growers that sell 25-50lb bags which range in the $2-4/lb range, so very reasonable and some even have different varieties and colors. They are often sold out though probably due to high demand and not an unlimited supply. I have a feeling both the Trader Joe's can and the dry beans I got from the grocery store were grown overseas.

## Where does the dried bean lose to the canned bean in price

I've seen fancier dried beans or heirloom beans cost $6-10/lb. Where does the inexpensive can of beans beat that price? At $6/lb for dried beans the cooked cost is about 15.4 cents/ounce or $2.47/lb. At $8/lb it's about 20.1 cents/ounce or $3.29/lb. 

## Nutrition

Garbanzo beans are quite nutritious and taste good, even a lot of kids like them. They have a low glycemic index (good for diabetes prevention), they have good fiber to aid digestion and intestinal health, and they can lower your LDL cholesterol amongst a million other things. Good snack if you're trying to eat healthy and avoid medications.

Per cooked cup, garbanzo beans provide 269 calories, 14.5g of protein, 4.25g of fat, 45g of carbohydrates, 12.5g of fiber, 282mcg of folate (B9), 1.7mg of manganese, 0.58mg of copper, 4.7mg of iron, 275.5mg of phosphorus, 78.7mg of magnesium, 477mg of potassium, and smaller amounts of vitamins B6, B1, B2, B3, B5, C, E, and K.

## Bean conclusion:

It was interesting to see the actual numbers and dried beans win out vs canned beans at the value pricing for organics. Even $8/lb dried beans only comes out to about $3.29/lb cooked which compared to a lot of other foods is a great deal. So if they are thoughtfully grown or a specific variety or you don't want a huge bag even at $8/lb dry it's still a fair price just not the cheapest. 

The convenience of canned beans is also pretty good. No soaking, cooking, or electricity cost and if you need them the same day they're ready to go right away, they're easy to store and last a long time in storage too. 

Bottom line, if you want the most bang for your buck go with dried beans, the instant pot makes cooking them so easy too. I just soaked overnight and then 10 mins high pressure, make sure you have enough water in there. Then eat them all!
