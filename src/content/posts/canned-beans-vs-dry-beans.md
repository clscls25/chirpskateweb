I've always wondered which was cheaper, dried or canned beans. I never bothered to actually measure everything. Until today! 

I assumed the dry beans were going to be cheaper overall. You have to do a lot more work, soak, cook, there had to be a payoff.

I bought 2 cans of Trader Joe's organic garbanzo beans for $1.19 each. I also bought some organic dry garbanzo beans at a local grocery store for $3.79/lb.

## Time to cook the beans

I cooked 2 cups of dry garbanzos which weighed 14.1 ounces. At $3.79/lb these 2 cups cost about $3.34. After cooking it weighed 34.3 ounces. So the dried added 6.1 ounces of water weight, and 34.3 ounces for $3.34 comes out to 9.7 cents/ounce ($1.55/lb). Wow only $1.55/lb, tastes good, nutritious, healthy, beans should be a staple for a lot of people.

The canned garbanzos said it weighed 15.5 ounces and I assumed that was just the beans but that weight actually includes the water in the can too. The can with the beans weighed 17.9 ounces and the canned beans drained weighed 9.6 ounces. At $1.19 for 9.6 ounces that comes out to 12.4 cents/ounce ($1.98/lb). Still really good, and under $2/lb, hard to beat.

## The price for convenience

It's hard to beat the convenience of canned beans, no soak time, no cooking, just open the can and rinse and you're ready to go. This convenience in this case costs about 43 cents per pound (about 28% more) for ready to go beans. Not bad, and not going to break the bank.

The canned Trader Joe's beans are surely some of the cheaper organic canned beans available. I've seen cans cost $2-4 regularly at various grocery stores. At $2/can it would be 20.1 cents/ounce ($3.22/lb), $3/can 31.3 cents/ounce ($5.00/lb), and $4/can 41.7 cents/ounce ($6.67/lb).

## Dried beans in bulk can be even cheaper

$3.79/lb is a pretty good price for organic dried beans. I've seen specialty organic dried beans go for $6.00/lb. I think if you really searched you might be able to get a 25lb bag of organic garbanzos for $50-75, so that would be $2-3/lb instead of the $3.79 I paid. That could drop the price to close to $1/lb cooked.

## Where does the expensive dried bean lose to the canned bean in price

I've seen fancier dried beans or heirloom beans cost $6-10/lb. Where does the inexpensive can of beans beat that price? At $6/lb for dried beans the cooked cost is about 15.4 cents/ounce or $2.47/lb. At $8/lb it's about 20.1 cents/lb or $3.29/lb.

## Bean conclusion:

It was interesting to see the actual numbers and dried beans win out vs canned beans at the value pricing for organics. Even $8/lb dried beans only comes out to about $3.29/lb cooked which compared to a lot of other foods is a great deal. The convenience of canned beans is also pretty high. No soaking, cooking, or electricity cost and if you need them the same day they're ready to go right away, they're easy to store and last a long time in storage too. Bottom line, if you want the most bang for your buck go with dried beans but can't really go wrong either way.
